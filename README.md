# 📊 E-Commerce Sales, Customers & Products Analysis (Power BI)

## 📌 Project Overview
This project presents a complete business analysis of a UK-based e-commerce company using **Power BI**.  
The dataset contains transactional data from **2011**, including online retail sales, customers, products and returns.

The goal is to understand **how products, customers and sales interact** in order to identify growth opportunities, profitability risks and optimization strategies.

The project is structured into **three analytical layers**:
1. Products  
2. Customers  
3. Sales  

Each layer answers real business questions used by management teams in retail and e-commerce.

---

## 📂 Dataset
The dataset used in this project is the **Online Retail Dataset** (UK e-commerce), which contains:

- Transaction-level data from **2011**
- Invoices and invoice dates
- Product descriptions
- Quantities and prices
- Customer IDs
- Cancelled transactions (returns)

It represents the activity of a real online retail company selling giftware and household products.

---

## 🎯 Business Objectives
The main objectives of this project are:

- Identify which products truly drive revenue
- Understand customer loyalty and purchasing behavior
- Detect profitability risks (returns, low-margin products)
- Analyze sales performance by time (day, hour, seasonality)
- Provide actionable recommendations for growth

---

## 📦 1. Product Analysis

### Business Questions
- Are sales concentrated in a few best-selling products?
- Which products are bought together?
- Which products generate high sales but also high returns?
- Which items should be promoted, reviewed or removed?

### Key Insights
- Sales are **highly distributed**: the top 20 products generate only **15% of total revenue**, meaning the business does not depend on a few best sellers.
- Most cross-selling happens **within the same category** (bags), showing a functional rather than strategic buying pattern.
- Some cross-category combinations (e.g. *cake stand + cups*) reveal strong **bundling opportunities**.
- A small number of products have **extreme return rates (95–100%)**, while the average return rate is only **7%**, creating a significant hidden profitability problem.

---

## 👥 2. Customer Analysis

### Business Questions
- How many customers are loyal vs occasional?
- How often do customers repurchase?
- Which products attract new customers?

### Key Insights
- **75% of customers** have made **five or fewer purchases**, showing a strong dependence on new customers rather than loyal ones.
- The average time between purchases is **60 days**, indicating a long repurchase cycle.
- The products most often chosen as a **first purchase** are also among the most sold products overall, making them key **entry products** to the catalog.

---

## 💰 3. Sales Performance

### Business Questions
- What is the average order value?
- Which days and hours generate the most revenue?
- How do sales evolve over the year?

### Key Insights
- The average order value is **376**, indicating a **high-value transaction model**.
- Revenue is concentrated during **weekdays**, especially on **Thursday and Tuesday**.
- **No sales occur on Saturdays**, suggesting operational or channel inactivity.
- Sales grow strongly from **September onwards**, peaking in **November**, showing clear **seasonality**.
- The **afternoon (12–18h)** is the most important conversion period, followed by the morning, with very low activity at night.

---

## 🧠 Global Business Conclusion
The e-commerce operates with:
- High-value orders
- A strong dependence on new customers
- A clear set of products acting as entry points
- Strong seasonal and time-based sales patterns

At the same time, profitability is being impacted by **a small number of products with extremely high return rates**, and customer retention represents the biggest growth opportunity.

---

## 📈 Strategic Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following actions would have the highest business impact:

- **Review or remove products with return rates above 80%** to immediately improve profitability.
- Create **bundles and cross-selling strategies** between complementary categories (e.g. cake stands + cups).
- Implement **customer retention campaigns before day 60** to shorten the repurchase cycle.
- Focus **marketing and promotions on Thursdays and in the afternoon**, when conversion is highest.
- Prioritize **Q4 (September–November)** for campaigns, inventory and budget allocation.
